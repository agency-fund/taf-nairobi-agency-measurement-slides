What is agency · an example theoretical framework

Agency needs to be defined to the context and measured as an outcome

It might be helpful to think of agency in terms of a theoretical framework. For example, in social cognitive theory, people are agents who intentionally shape their circumstances. Agency has four core elements in this framework:

1

Intentionality

Forming plans and committing to act on them.

Example · goal commitment

The learner states a business goal in her own words and commits to a concrete next step before the session ends.

2

Forethought

Setting goals and anticipating the likely outcomes of actions.

Example · planning and tradeoffs

She weighs two ideas against each other, asking what each would cost, earn, and risk in her local market.

3

Self-reactiveness

Monitoring progress and adjusting course along the way.

Example · course correction

She returns with progress updates and revises the plan when the first supplier falls through, instead of abandoning the goal.

4

Self-reflectiveness

Evaluating one's own thinking, capability, and results.

Example · calibrated self-belief

After the business pitch, she reviews what worked and what did not, and updated her mental models to develop the business plan.

Source: Bandura (2001), Social cognitive theory: an agentic perspective.
