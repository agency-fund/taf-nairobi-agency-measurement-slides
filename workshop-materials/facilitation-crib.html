Calibrate setup (before the day)

  • Create 3 annotation tasks, one per domain, from workshop-data/*.csv (10 conversations each, keep upload order: it is the intended coding order, and lengths vary on purpose — power users to two-line drive-bys).
  • Configure 4 binary evaluators + 1 comment field per task, wording exactly as in workshop-data/ANSWER_KEY.md (the flywheel, one question per node: belief shift / specific action / life-outcome link / uses past experience). Answer options: Yes / No only — binary on purpose, borderline cases force a choice and that is the disagreement fuel. Reasoning optional.
  • DONE: 20 labeller links per task pre-generated (labelling jobs EDU/HEA/AGR-01–20; tokens in gitignored workshop-data/annotator_links.json) and collected in the Google claim sheet (short link bit.ly/44QhF4e), reached via the QR on the clock screen. Sharing must be "Anyone with the link: Editor" — verify from an incognito window. The name column doubles as the seat-code → real-name map for the debrief (Calibrate shows annotators as EDU-07 etc.).
  • Pre-run the LLM evaluator on all 30 items with the same four questions. The debrief reveal must not depend on a live API call.
  • Open a labeller link on your own phone: confirm the labeling UI is usable on mobile.
  • Dry-run the full loop with 2-3 colleagues: code, adjudicate, read the agreement view. Time it.

Asks for Aman

  • Bulk generation of ~60 labeller links — done via API (see claim sheet).
  • Confirm the agreement summary view can be shown per task on a projector mid-session, refreshed live.
  • Confirm an evaluation run on annotation items can be triggered (or pre-run) and compared against human labels in one view.

Contingencies

Wifi dies: print the transcripts beforehand (one set per table; items 01-06 of each domain suffice on paper). Coding happens on paper against slide 12; adjudication and debrief proceed; slide 13 walks the same three beats without live numbers, and the Noora Health experience carries the calibration point.
  • Turnout much smaller than expected: drop to two domains (education + whichever fits the room); merge tables to 4+ coders each so agreement stats still mean something.
  • Turnout much larger: links run out at 20/domain; pairs share a link, which is fine: pair-consensus labels still disagree across pairs.
  • Sheet chaos (two names in one row, someone edits headers): announce "lowest unclaimed code in your block"; worst case assign code ranges to tables verbally ("table 1: EDU-01 to 06"). A dead or mis-clicked link: strike it through, take the next unclaimed row.
  • QR won't scan from the screen: read out or chat-drop bit.ly/44QhF4e (case-sensitive); it is also on the claim sheet's browser tab of your own laptop.
  • Running 10 min behind after the talk: cut coding to 8 min and adjudication to 5. Never cut the debrief: it carries the whole lesson.
  • A table finishes early: send them to item 03 (the ambiguous flagship) if they skipped it, or ask them to draft one improvement to any question's wording.

Debrief ammunition

Flagship disagreement items: HEA-03 Q4 is the centerpiece trap — "I stopped the syrup early and nothing happened" codes Yes on uses-past-experience AND contradicts clinical guidance: the flywheel turning is not the same as turning well. AGR-02 is the rubric-limits lesson — by the letter, the most dependent farmer scores Yes on Q2 and Q4; binary labels can't see ownership, only the open note can. The maybe-rule trio EDU-07 / HEA-07 / AGR-07: real belief shift (Q1 Yes), but "maybe i try" never codes Yes on Q2. The direction nuance: EDU-09 / HEA-09 / AGR-09 are negative loops — lost hope still codes Yes on Q1. The all-No trio: EDU-08 / HEA-08 / AGR-08 are drive-bys; all-No is correct, and anyone who finds agency there is the calibration conversation. The vicarious-experience pair HEA-05 / AGR-05: does someone ELSE's past (a brother's amputation, a neighbour's inspection pass) count as "past experience"? The rubric is silent — let tables fight it out.

AGR-05 provocation if discussion stalls: Esther refuses to adapt anything in a contract year — "i am not experimenting in a contract year." Is deference under high stakes low agency, or exactly the rational move?
